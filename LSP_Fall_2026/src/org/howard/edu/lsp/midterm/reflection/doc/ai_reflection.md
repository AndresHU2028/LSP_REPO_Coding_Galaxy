Benefits:
This semester, AI has helped my learning by showing code implementations that I otherwise would not be able to/would need
extensive time to learn how to do. The primary language I understand is Python. To successfully complete a 
lot of the assignments that allowed AI, I needed to use a conversation with an LLM to even begin to understand where
to start in Java, such as with Assignment #3. Additionally, while I am still improving on it, on occasion I don't feel 
like I have the analytical brain when it comes to Computer Science, specifically having o breakdown solutions into 
Classes, responsibilities, etc. AI has proven to be a very useful tool in doing so. For example, Assignment #4 of this 
course allowed the usage of AI for the OO decomposition of a scenario. It was helpful to me to have this tool to break it
down as I otherwise would not know where to start to even find the Classes, Attributes, and Behaviors for the "Event
Registration Business" description.

Challenges:
A situation, as alluded to earlier in my development log for Question 3 of this midterm, where AI was unhelpful was when
it made the choice on its own to only show a snippet of the refactored solution for Question 3 and not the full
implementation. I initially brushed past it, but when I looked closely at the requirements for Question 3, I noticed that
what Gemini gave me did not factor in at all. I asked it why it made this choice, and its response was along the lines of
"I'm sorry, I just chose to make that choice". Outside of this course, in personal use, I've seen AI do a similar thing
where randomly it decides to make a choice that I did not ask. This showed me the importance on solely using AI as a 
tool. As if I just left the answer it initially generated for me, I would have messed up my implementation. Only when I
looked in depth at the requirements for Question 3 I noticed this error.

Moving Forward:
Going forward, I intend to use AI as I have been and as some of my courses have allowed: a tool. Specifically, use it not simply as a catch-all problem solver, but use it to explain the concept/assignment I may not understand, and then comb
through it to better grasp the solution. An example of how I do this (at least for this class) is giving AI initially the
entire requirements, and then looking through its generated solution line-by-line to see if it works. And along the way 
if I have questions, I ask the AI to explain it. That way, it helps me with the assignment, but I also don't risk the
cognitive offloading that can come with using AI without any understanding of what it gives you.
